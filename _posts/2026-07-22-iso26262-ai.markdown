## *AI Causes the Layman (me) to Arrive*

I had an idea for a tool that would help me complete one of the most ardious parts of my tasks in ISO26262[^1] compliance; documenting my code reviews in an excel sheet with any findings and scheduling a meeting with the developer. Why do I use an Excel spreadsheet, some dated internal tool, or something like that? 

I wrote a script in Python that could output a spreadsheet in with all the details I need but really dislike trying to create a UI in procedural libraries like PySide6. And - much like every other office worker - my boss keeps nagging me to try out AI, so I decided to have it spit out a UI using Qt and PySide6. The next thing you know, I spoke with a co-worker about it and they end up getting their manager on board too, and I'm standing in front of nine people presenting my idea for the case.

Right out the gate, our compliance officer prefaced his response "I apprecaite the effort but..." and I knew I was going to get lambasted.

## The ISO26262 Promise

The reason my application was rejected was becasuse there is a ISO26262 dictates all software must start as requirements, then tests or acceptance criteria are created against those requirements. Only then can you begin on the implementation work. 
I had no clue this applied to tools that don't touch the code like your ticketing system, calibration integration tool, or requirements management system. 

Taking a step back, this makes a lot of sense because a deterministic system is a safe system. Every time you have a finding you must be almost certain her findings were correctly saved. I had the thought in the back of my mind, "how do we write compliant code?"
