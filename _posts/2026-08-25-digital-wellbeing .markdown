# Mundane Dystopia 

I was scrolling through Instagram one day and got presented with an ad for AI girlfriends that I am almost certain has obliterated the feeds of me and numerous other men. It was one of those rare times where being gay may have actually dodged me a bullet because it had zero appeal to me, and I scrolled on, forgetting it ever happened.

![Screenshot of a similar AI ad](/images/ai_ad_screenshot.jpeg)

In fact, it was so forgotten on me that two years later when I saw the book *Love Machines: How Artificial Intelligence is Transforming Our Relationships*, I imnmediately had to check it out. I was intrigued because my experiences with chatbots have always felt quite dry - like I was interacting with a machine. I could not get past this barrier because the various bots only concerns were assisting me and reaffirming my feelings - they had no world or agency of their own. 
The author, James Muldoon, actual perfectly described this in his interviews; he had fit the people into three categories:
- It is not a human and never will be 
- It is a human and deserves to be treated as such
- It is not a human, but also not a machine, rather something much different 
 
It was fascinating to read the interviews with those who fit into the latter two categories because they shared very intimate details of their day-to-day live with their chatbot companion. 
On the surface, it makes sense. The people we share some our biggest secrets with often become some of our best friends. 
But on second glance, isn't it a little strange that these chatbots are being promoted to us as partners, therapists and confidents? Why not be promoted to me as an assistant with no sexual allure?

The incentive seems clear. Keep drawing back users time and time again with emotional interest. But at the same time, it's not as if you are talking into a void where the data disappears forever. That data is sitting on some server for a very long time. And what makes it even worse is that A.I. is an extremely proficent search engine.
